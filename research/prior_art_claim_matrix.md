# R7 Prior-Art Claim Matrix

## Scope and evidence standard

This matrix audits the exact claims in [the formal model](formal_model.md) and [the R6 profile engine notes](detection_profile_engine.md). “Exact location” means a section/page/equation verified in the cited text or an explicit statement that only metadata/abstract was accessible. A result derived here from a source's definitions is identified as our derivation; it is not attributed to an uninspected theorem. “Not located” records the limits of this search and is not evidence of absence.

Primary and authoritative material inspected includes Verhoeff's full 1969 monograph, Hamming's freely accessible three-page extract and publisher record, the official ISO preview, Luhn's patent, ISBN.org's FAQ, Schulz's accessible chapter on check-character systems, and the Oxford abstract for Abdel-Ghaffar. Gumm's and Damm's full articles were not accessible in the sources checked; abstract-level descriptions are not treated as proof of their detailed mathematics.

## General weighted checksum connection

For a check expression (S(x)=\sum_iw_ix_i\pmod m), changing only coordinate (i) from (a) to (b) changes the check expression by

\[
\Delta=(b-a)w_i\pmod m.
\]

If positions (i,j) contain (a,b) and are exchanged, the change is

\[
\Delta=(b-a)(w_i-w_j)\pmod m,
\]

up to the chosen sign convention. These are direct algebraic identities. Verhoeff's treatment of weighted sums explicitly uses the fact that nonzero position weights support complete single-error detection and that transposition detection depends on the relevant weights being distinct modulo the check modulus (for the particular decimal/check-digit setting and digit domain; §2.3.3, pp. 54–55). Parhami's equation (13.2.chksm) and UPC solution state the general weighted-check viewpoint and derive its example-specific conditions.

The project is exactly the plain-residue specialization (w_k=10^k), with no appended check digit or selected valid-code subset. For adjacent positions, (w_{k+1}-w_k=9\cdot10^k). The reduced gcd divisors are the project's concise way to state when that weighted delta vanishes modulo (m). This is a transparent specialization of known weighted-check arithmetic, not an independent new checksum family.

## Search boundary

Queries covered linear/modular/weighted check digits, decimal error-detecting codes, arbitrary number-system check digits, substitution/transposition conditions, ISBN/mod-11, Verhoeff, Gumm, Damm, error rates, equivalent check systems, and digit-difference counts. The citation trail through Verhoeff's bibliography and Schulz's group-system chapter showed extensive neighboring work. No searched source disclosed the exact generic (W(h)) notation or the precise two stabilization formulas, but source coverage is incomplete, historical terminology varies, and some relevant primary texts remain inaccessible. Consequently these rows remain **not located**, not claims of novelty.

The exact finite experiment (moduli 2–30, lengths 1–4) and its artifact-level reproducibility are also not a literature priority claim. Verhoeff had already analyzed rates for decimal codes and documented a search program; this rules out broad claims that computation or enumeration of decimal error-detecting schemes is itself new. The project may still have repository-specific reproducibility value, which is different from a demonstrated scholarly contribution.
