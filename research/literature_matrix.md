# R3 Focused Literature Matrix

## Scope and method

This is a focused review of the claims in [the formal model](formal_model.md), not a systematic review of every check-digit or coding-theory publication. Searches used conceptual variants including decimal check-digit codes, single substitutions, adjacent transpositions, modular and weighted checksums, modulus 11, gcd and base powers, and error-detecting codes. Primary papers, a monograph, a standard, an original patent, and institutional records were prioritized. Wikipedia, blogs, Stack Exchange, commercial check-digit pages, and search snippets were discovery aids only, not evidence.

The project's object is a fixed-width decimal string with `N(x) = sum(x_k * 10^k)`, checked by the plain residue `N(x) mod m`. It appends no check digit. Events include a source string and a specified edit; leading zeros remain part of the fixed-width string. Many comparison schemes instead append a check character and use position weights, nonlinear transforms, groups, or quasigroups.

**Classification key:** A = directly established by a source under the same definitions; B = established special case; C = straightforward consequence of established mathematics or coding theory; D = related work with materially different definitions or construction; E = equivalent result not located in this search (not a novelty claim); F = relationship cannot be determined from accessible source material. Classifications describe each source's relationship to the project claim, not whether the project proves its own theorem.

## Research gap and assessment

### A. What is definitely established before this project?

Decimal check-digit design, single-substitution and transposition error models, code-theoretic bounds/constructions, group-based check-digit methods, and standardized check-character systems all predate this project. Verhoeff's 1969 monograph is direct evidence of substantial decimal-code study, including a survey, error tests, analytical chapters, and computational search. The broad idea of systematic computational analysis of decimal error-detecting codes is therefore not new.

### B. What does the project independently derive?

Within its plain-residue model, the project derives the two delta identities, gcd-based necessary-and-sufficient conditions, fixed-position and fixed-length thresholds, all-finite-length prime-factor criteria, and exact event-count formulas. "Independently derives" describes this project's proof, not priority in the literature.

### C. What appears to follow directly from known theory?

The gcd lemma is Euclid's lemma applied to a product. The `>9` threshold follows from the finite decimal difference set. Endpoint and prime-factor conditions follow from factor cancellation in `10^k`, and `W(h)` is a direct ordered-pair count. These are elementary consequences; the evidence does not support framing them as deep new theory.

### D. What appears insufficiently covered by located literature?

This review did not locate an accessible publication stating the exact all-finite-length factorization criteria for the *unweighted plain integer residue* `N(x) mod m`, nor an exact matching enumeration/reconciliation over this repository's modulus and length grid. This is a bounded search result, not evidence that no such source exists. Verhoeff's computational search precedent also precludes a broad claim such as "first computational study of decimal error detection."

### E. What remains uncertain?

Whether the exact characterization or an equivalent formulation appears in a less obvious checksum, number-theory, or coding-theory source; whether comparable plain-residue sweeps exist; and theorem-level details in inaccessible parts of Gumm, Damm, Hamming, Verhoeff, and ISO sources. This is a focused review, not an exhaustive or publication-grade systematic review.

### F. Recommended next research direction

**One recommended next action:** conduct a citation-chained, full-text prior-art review focused on linear positional checksum codes over arbitrary bases, tracing the relevant references from Verhoeff and the primary articles by Gumm and Abdel-Ghaffar, then compare their exact code definitions against `N(x) mod m`. Record full-text access and theorem locations before making any novelty or contribution claim.

## Claims that must not be made

- Do not claim that the all-length characterization is novel, first, or absent from all literature.
- Do not describe this computation as the first systematic computational study of decimal error detection.
- Do not treat ISBN-10, ISO/IEC 7064, Luhn, Verhoeff, or Damm as the same algorithm as plain `N(x) mod m`.
- Do not use a general group/quasigroup abstract as proof of every property of one specific decimal check-digit table.
- Do not extend the modulus-11 result beyond single substitutions and unequal adjacent transpositions under the project's definitions.
- Do not treat finite sweep results as proof for all moduli or lengths, or event-weighted rates as real-world error probabilities.
- Do not cite tertiary summaries, search snippets, or inaccessible full text as if their detailed technical claims were verified.
